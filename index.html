Research focus

Three connected lines of inquiry.

Thesis & collaborative research

River sediment & AMD microbiomes

Investigating temperature-driven shifts in methanogenic and methanotrophic communities in Kanawha River sediments, alongside collaborative acid mine drainage (AMD) remediation research. Integrating molecular biology, bioinformatics, and environmental chemistry to characterize microbial community responses.

Temperature, methane cycling & remediation

My thesis focuses on how seasonal and within-season temperature variation influences methanogenic and methanotrophic communities in Kanawha River sediments. Collaborative work includes examining microbial ecology and environmental chemistry before and after acid mine drainage remediation, as well as investigating how stresses such as salt and coal influence sediment methane production.

02
Plant microbiome dataset analysis

Plant-associated microbiomes

Analyzing plant-associated microbiome datasets to investigate how environmental stresses and treatments shape bacterial and fungal community composition and predicted functional potential, using bioinformatics and machine learning. Current work includes an integrated reanalysis of cotton rhizosphere bacterial and fungal communities using K-means clustering.

Amplicon sequence analysisFunctional predictionK-means clusteringStatistical validationBioinformatics
Community composition & functional potential

I analyze plant-associated microbiome datasets to explore bacterial and fungal community responses to environmental stresses and treatments. My cotton rhizosphere reanalysis uses the public dataset PRJNA875969 and combines amplicon sequence analysis, compositional transformation, K-means clustering, statistical validation, and functional prediction. Predicted functions provide hypotheses rather than direct measurements of microbial activity.

03
Methods & research interests

AI/ML in microbial ecology

Driven by a strong curiosity about AI/ML, I explore its application to agricultural and environmental microbiology, where complex communities contain many poorly characterized microorganisms. My interests include unsupervised learning to uncover community patterns without predefined labels and supervised approaches to predict ecological responses when suitable labeled data are available.

Ecological predictionPattern discoveryDimensionality reductionPredictive modeling
Pattern discovery & ecological prediction

High-dimensional microbiome data can contain complex relationships that are difficult to resolve. I am interested in unsupervised approaches for discovering community structure without predefined labels, and supervised approaches for predicting ecological responses when suitable labeled data are available. Evaluating stability, generalization, and biological relevance is central to interpreting these models.

Why research

Curiosity that connects
the field and the data.

I want to understand what microbial communities are doing, why they change, and what those changes mean for the environment.

As a graduate researcher in biotechnology at West Virginia State University, I’m developing my work at the intersection of environmental and agricultural microbiology, molecular biotechnology, and bioinformatics.

My enthusiasm for research comes from connecting ecological questions with the tools needed to investigate them—from sediment and soil microbiomes to computational models of community structure.

Future research direction

Building toward a PhD in agricultural microbiology and plant–microbe interactions.

I’m interested in understanding how plant-associated microbial communities influence plant health, contribute to disease suppression, and respond to environmental stresses. My goal is to combine molecular biology, metagenomics, and metatranscriptomics with bioinformatics and ML to investigate these interactions, connecting my background in environmental and agricultural microbiology with questions in agricultural systems.
